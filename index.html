DPonTrain: dynamic pricing for Indian Railways

I took airline-style revenue management and pointed it at a train. Using about 736,000 real bookings for the Punjab Mail (Firozpur to Mumbai, 52 stops), I forecast demand per departure, built a booking simulator, and compared ways of setting fares on departures the models had never seen. A plain forecast plus a dynamic program beat reinforcement learning, and I wrote up why.

+2% to +14%revenue per departure vs a fixed fare
95–100%of the perfect-information ceiling
+8.5%more with network bid prices
6.3%forecast error (MAPE) on unseen months
simple math beat RL herea simple blend beat boosting

How it works
~736K bookingsDemand forecastBooking simulatorDP fare optimizerRevenue vs fixed fare
  • Forecast: a blend of weekday average and same weekday last year sets demand before booking opens; a pickup model sharpens it from bookings already on hand. Tested on months it never saw.
  • Simulator: daily Poisson bookings that follow the real booking curve, with price sensitivity tested as scenarios because fares never changed in the data.
  • Optimizer: a dynamic program over days left and seats left picks the fare, and a Gamma–Poisson update re-forecasts demand as bookings arrive.
  • Network: LP and DP bid prices price 36 origin–destination journeys over 8 legs of the route.
  • Fair test: 365 unseen departures, with the same simulated customers for every policy.

Bandits vs. A/B testing for news recommendation

A course project in CSE at Michigan. I built a neural click simulator on MIND-large news data (2.2M impressions, AUC 0.733) and measured how much A/B testing gives up against adaptive methods. Then I looked inside the policies with sparse-autoencoder features, and gave an LLM agent a statistics tool to see if it would choose better.

83%less regret than A/B testing with tuned UCB
66%less with Thompson sampling
44%less in LinUCB with Gemma-3 embeddings
39%less for the LLM agent with the tool
regret = clicks you missed
Code peek inside the repo
How it works
MIND-large logsNeural click simulatorA/B test vs banditsCumulative regret
  • Simulator: a neural click model trained on 2.2M MIND-large impressions (global AUC 0.733) stands in for real readers.
  • Policies: A/B testing against tuned UCB, Thompson sampling and LinUCB over 100K rounds.
  • Content features: Gemma-3 embeddings in LinUCB under article turnover, and Gemma Scope 2 sparse-autoencoder features to interpret what the policies use.
  • LLM agent: a Gemma-3-12B agent choosing articles, with and without a statistics tool.

Synapse: a study planner that syncs with Canvas

A team project for CSE 593 (HCI) at Michigan. Our five-person team designed a phone app that turns Canvas assignments into short study sessions and reshuffles them when life happens. We went from a survey pilot and contextual inquiries to personas, storyboards and sketches, then built a high-fidelity prototype in Figma Make for user testing.
